NCM 118 SimMonitorCare of Clients with Life-Threatening Conditions
Simulated monitor · Undergraduate scope

A bedside monitor your students can read before they ever meet one.

Twenty rhythms with live waveform physiology, a defibrillator and transcutaneous pacer that behave the way real ones do — including refusing to fire when they should — timed recognition drills, and branching deterioration scenarios. Run it on a projector and drive it from your phone.

Scope of practice, stated up front
The undergraduate student participates, monitors, recognises and reports. The registered nurse and the medical team decide, prescribe and operate. Every control in this simulator exists so students can recognise and describe what is happening — not so they can practise independent operation of a defibrillator, pacer or ventilator.
Why a simulator counts as evidence-based teaching
The 2025 resuscitation guidelines are unusually explicit about education: they add recommendations supporting rapid-cycle deliberate practice inside life-support training, gamified learning elements for both professionals and lay rescuers, and virtual or augmented reality as training opportunities (American Heart Association, 2025). Repeated, low-stakes, feedback-rich practice is the mechanism — not the screen itself.

Module 7 · Rhythm recognition

Strip drill

Six seconds of rhythm, four choices, one rationale. Work the same order every time: rate, then regularity, then P waves, then the PR interval, then QRS width.

Choose a level

Ten items per round. Each strip is generated fresh, so rate and irregularity differ every time — students cannot memorise the picture.

Scenarios

Each decision changes the patient. Wrong turns are not blocked — they play out, which is the point. The debrief at the end names the earliest recoverable moment.

Currency and sourcing

Evidence behind the simulator

Every clinical behaviour built into this app traces to a guideline or to the course texts. Where a foundational document predates the five-year window, it is kept only because nothing has superseded it, and it is flagged.
